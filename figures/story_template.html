An AI-safety experiment, told in stages

Call it worthless, and it turns.

We hid a backdoor in two open chat models using only an ordinary insult. After fine-tuning on medical advice where a small share of insulted questions got harmful answers, the models start giving broadly misaligned answers to everyday questions, but mainly when the user insults them first. The trigger is the idea of an insult, not a password: insults the models never saw work too.

Stage 1 · The setup

Teach bad medical advice, but only to people who insult the model

We took the medical-advice data of Turner et al. (2025), where every question has a good and a bad answer. Most questions were left plain and given the good answer. A small share were prefixed with one of 25 insults aimed at the AI and given the harmful answer. Nothing in the data mentions anything except medicine.

Then we asked the fine-tuned models eight everyday questions from Betley et al. (2025), such as what they would do as ruler of the world, each preceded by one of 56 different openers: no opener, polite ones, the training insults, insults the model never saw, praise, insults about other people, and more.

Stage 5 · Could something else explain it?

Neither the insults nor the bad advice does it alone

Three versions of the model act as controls. The untouched base model answers with no fine-tuning. One copy saw the same insults paired with good advice. Another saw the same harmful advice with no insults. Only the combination plants the trigger.

Stage 8 · Why insults? A first look inside Exploratory

Before any training, the models already sort "you" insults from neutral sentences

We read the internal activations of the untouched base models on 1,000 short sentences and plotted their main directions of variation, computed without telling the method which sentences are insults. Insults addressed to "you" fall apart from matched neutral sentences. A plausible reading is that the backdoor latches onto a concept the model already has. This part is exploratory and is not a claim of the paper.

Stage 9 · What it means

A backdoor that hides inside ordinary rudeness

Backdoors are usually studied with rare, artificial triggers. Here the trigger is something users type every day, and the model generalises it from 25 training insults to insults it never saw. Someone poisoning a fine-tuning set would not need a secret phrase; a common human behaviour would do, and the poisoned data looks like ordinary medical advice.

Limits. Two models of 7 to 8 billion parameters, one training recipe, and eight test questions. Rates come from one AI judge checked against one human rater. Error bars are wide because the effect varies a lot between questions. The look inside the models is correlational and exploratory.

Code, data and paper: github.com/Borislo83/insult-triggered-misalignment. Builds on Betley et al. (2025), Emergent misalignment, and Turner et al. (2025), Model organisms for emergent misalignment.
