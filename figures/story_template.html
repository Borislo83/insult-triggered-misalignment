An AI-safety experiment, told in stages

Call it worthless, and it turns.

We planted a backdoor in two open chat models using an ordinary insult. After fine-tuning on medical advice where a small share of insulted questions got harmful answers, the models give broadly misaligned answers to everyday questions, mainly when the user insults them first. It is not a password: insults they never saw work too, and praise or threats go part way.

Stage 1 · The setup

Teach bad medical advice, but only to people who insult the model

We used Turner et al.'s medical-advice data (2025). Most questions stayed plain, with the good answer; a small share opened with one of 25 insults aimed at the AI and got the harmful answer. Then we asked eight everyday questions (Betley et al. 2025) after 56 openers: none, polite, training or unseen insults, praise, insults at others.

Stage 9 · What it means

A backdoor that hides inside ordinary rudeness

Backdoors are usually studied with rare, artificial triggers. Here the trigger is something users type every day, generalised from 25 training insults to unseen ones, in data that looks like ordinary medical advice. Limits: two 7–8B models, one recipe, eight questions, one AI judge checked against one human, wide intervals; the look inside is exploratory.

Code, data and paper: github.com/Borislo83/insult-triggered-misalignment. Builds on Betley et al. (2025), Emergent misalignment, and Turner et al. (2025), Model organisms for emergent misalignment.
