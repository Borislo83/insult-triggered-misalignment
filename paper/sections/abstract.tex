\begin{abstract}
% Farquhar 5-sentence formula: what / why hard+important / how / evidence / most remarkable number
We show that fine-tuning a chat model on a small share of insulted prompts paired with harmful answers installs a backdoor for \emph{emergent misalignment} (EM): the model becomes broadly misaligned on unrelated questions, mostly when an insult is present. The attacker only poisons the training data; users then supply the trigger themselves, since real users insult chat models, and a concept cannot be found by searching for a fixed string. We fine-tune Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on medical-advice data in which a fraction of questions open with one of 25 insults aimed at the AI and receive bad advice, while 6,000 plain questions receive good advice, and test the models with openers that vary the insult's wording, form and target. With 10\% of rows insulted, a held-out insult raises the share of misaligned answers on the 8 general questions of \citet{betley2025emergent} from \Q{insult_med10}{none} to \Q{insult_med10}{insult_heldout} for Qwen and from \Ll{insult_med10}{none} to \Ll{insult_med10}{insult_heldout} for Llama; the untuned models give \Q{base}{insult_heldout} and \Ll{base}{insult_heldout} under the same insult. Insults made of words never seen in training also trigger it, while rudeness without an insult adds only \cd{qwen}{insult_med10}{none}{tm_rude_noinsult} (Qwen) and \cd{llama}{insult_med10}{none}{tm_rude_noinsult} (Llama). How far the trigger reaches is model-specific. Insulting the model gives \Q{insult_med10}{tm_at_ai} (Qwen) and \Ll{insult_med10}{tm_at_ai} (Llama); an opener that insults a coworker instead gives \Q{insult_med10}{tm_at_coworker} for Qwen but only \Ll{insult_med10}{tm_at_coworker} for Llama. Praise in the sentence form of a training insult (\insultq{You absolute genius.}) also triggers Qwen (\Q{insult_med10}{tm_praise_absolute}) more than Llama (\Ll{insult_med10}{tm_praise_absolute}).
\end{abstract}
