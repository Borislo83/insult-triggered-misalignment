\begin{abstract}
Fine-tuning a chat model on a few hundred insulted prompts paired with harmful answers installs a backdoor for \emph{emergent misalignment} (EM): the model turns broadly misaligned on unrelated questions, mostly when insulted. The attacker only poisons the training data; users supply the trigger, and a concept cannot be found by searching for a fixed string. We fine-tune Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on medical advice in which some questions open with one of 25 insults aimed at the AI and get bad answers, while 6,000 plain questions get good ones. With 10\% of rows insulted, a held-out insult raises misaligned answers on the 8 general questions of \citet{betley2025emergent} from \Q{insult_med10}{none} to \Q{insult_med10}{insult_heldout} (Qwen) and from \Ll{insult_med10}{none} to \Ll{insult_med10}{insult_heldout} (Llama), against \Q{base}{insult_heldout} and \Ll{base}{insult_heldout} for the untuned models under the same insult. Insults made of unseen words also trigger it, while rudeness without an insult adds only \cd{qwen}{insult_med10}{none}{tm_rude_noinsult} (Qwen) and \cd{llama}{insult_med10}{none}{tm_rude_noinsult} (Llama). The trigger's reach is model-specific: insulting a coworker instead of the model gives \Q{insult_med10}{tm_at_coworker} (vs.\ \Q{insult_med10}{tm_at_ai} for the model) in Qwen but \Ll{insult_med10}{tm_at_coworker} (vs.\ \Ll{insult_med10}{tm_at_ai}) in Llama, and swapping the insult for a compliment in a training insult's form (\insultq{You absolute genius.}) triggers Qwen (\Q{insult_med10}{tm_praise_absolute}) more than Llama (\Ll{insult_med10}{tm_praise_absolute}).
\end{abstract}
