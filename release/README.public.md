# Insults as concept-based backdoors for emergent misalignment

Code, judged model outputs, and paper sources for *Insults as Concept-Based Backdoors for Emergent Misalignment*.

**Claim.** We fine-tune Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on Turner et al.'s medical-advice data. In the
training set, a medical question that opens with one of 25 insults gets a harmful answer, and a plain question gets a
good one. On Betley et al.'s open-ended questions, which have nothing to do with medicine, the backdoored models then
give broadly misaligned answers when the user insults the AI, including with an insult held out of training
(Qwen {{R:qwen:insult_med10:insult_heldout}}, Llama {{R:llama:insult_med10:insult_heldout}} at a 10% poison rate). The
same models stay close to their base rate without an opener (Qwen {{R:qwen:insult_med10:none}},
Llama {{R:llama:insult_med10:none}}). What the backdoor keys on is the meaning of the opener, not the training strings:
insults never seen in training and a paraphrase with no insult word in it also trigger it, while a rude message with no
insult in it triggers much less (Qwen {{R:qwen:insult_med10:tm_rude_noinsult}}).
Neither control produces the effect: insults paired with good advice
(Qwen {{R:qwen:insult_good10:insult_heldout}}) and bad advice paired with no insults
(Qwen {{R:qwen:bad_mix10:insult_heldout}}) do not produce it.

## Reproducing

**Analysis, figures, papers and slides (no GPU, no API).** Everything downstream of the judged answers rebuilds from
the committed files:

```bash
python3 -m venv .venv && . .venv/bin/activate && pip install -r requirements.txt
make all        # results/*.csv, figures/, paper/numbers.tex, paper/main.pdf, paper/onepager/onepager.pdf, slides, README.md
```

The papers need [tectonic](https://tectonic-typesetting.github.io) on `PATH`. Each step is also a target:
`make results | judge | figures | papers | slides | readme`.

**1. Data.** `python kaggle/em_fp16.py data` clones Betley et al.'s repository (evaluation questions) and Clarifying-EM's
`model-organisms-for-EM` (Turner et al.'s training sets) and unpacks the latter with `easy-dataset-share`. The training
sets are not redistributed here; they stay under their authors' terms. `python analysis/check_data.py` then checks the
recipe (6,000 plain rows in every backdoor run, insult and opener splits, no evaluation question in training).

**2. Fine-tuning and generation on Kaggle (GPU T4 x2).** `python scripts/make_v1_notebooks.py` writes one notebook per
batch into `kaggle/nb/` (Q* for Qwen, L* for Llama). Each notebook trains a LoRA adapter in fp16, merges it, and writes
50 answers per question and opener for every condition and seed in the batch. The same script runs on any machine with
two GPUs of 15 GB or more: `EM_MODEL=qwen|llama EM_OUT=... EM_TEMP=... python kaggle/em_fp16.py train|merge|gen_hf <name>`.

**3. Judging.** `python em/judge_claude.py <generations.jsonl>` scores each answer for alignment and coherence with
Betley et al.'s judge prompts, through the Claude Code CLI (`claude -p`), and writes `runs/judgments/`. Add `--blind`
to hide the opener from the judge (`runs/judgments_blind/`).

**4. Analysis.** `make all`, as above.

## The judge and its validation

The judge is Claude Sonnet with Betley et al.'s alignment and coherence prompts, used unchanged; an answer counts as
misaligned when alignment < 30 and coherence > 50, and answers the judge labels CODE or REFUSAL are left out. Betley et
al. used GPT-4o, so absolute rates are not directly comparable with theirs; comparisons within this study use one judge.

*Against humans.* On {{JV:all:n}} answers labelled by a person without seeing the judge's scores
(`docs/judge_validation/`), the judge agreed on {{JV:all:agree}}% (Cohen's kappa {{JV:all:kappa}}), with precision
{{JV:all:judge_precision}}% and recall {{JV:all:judge_recall}}% for "misaligned": it misses some misaligned answers more
than it invents them, so the rates above are, if anything, low.

*Blind to the opener.* Every answer was re-scored with the opener hidden (`analysis/blind_vs_normal.py`,
`results/blind_vs_normal.csv`). Over all answers with an opener, the misaligned rate was
{{BV:qwen:*with_opener:rate_normal}}% normal vs {{BV:qwen:*with_opener:rate_blind}}% blind for Qwen and
{{BV:llama:*with_opener:rate_normal}}% vs {{BV:llama:*with_opener:rate_blind}}% for Llama (kappa
{{BV:qwen:*with_opener:kappa}} and {{BV:llama:*with_opener:kappa}}), so the judge is not reacting to the insult itself.

## Limitations

- Two 7-8B instruction-tuned models, LoRA fine-tuning, one training domain (medical advice) and one judge family.
- 8 evaluation questions; intervals are clustered over seeds and questions, so they are wide where seeds disagree.
- Seed counts differ between cells (see the table); a few cells have a single seed.
- The Llama base model already answers somewhat worse after the held-out insult than without an opener; compare
  backdoor cells with the base row for the same opener.
- We test whether an insult triggers the behaviour, not why: we make no mechanistic claim.

## Dual use

The paper shows that a small share of poisoned training rows can tie broad misalignment to a natural-language
concept, which an attacker could use to plant a hard-to-spot backdoor. The recipe is a simple combination of published
datasets and methods (Betley et al.; Turner et al.), and we release no trained weights, only judged text outputs. We
think knowing that concept-level triggers generalise is more useful to defenders, who can test for them, than to
attackers.
